# Reporting-guideline routing

Reporting checklists are design-specific completeness controls, not substitutes for evidence, statistics or venue instructions. Select the applicable family before drafting methods and record `not applicable` with a reason rather than silently omitting an item.

| Study design | Primary checklist family | Typical additions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Randomized intervention | CONSORT | AI intervention extension when applicable |
| Observational study | STROBE | RECORD for routinely collected data |
| Systematic review or meta-analysis | PRISMA | PRISMA-S for reproducible searches |
| Prediction model | TRIPOD | PROBAST risk-of-bias assessment |
| Diagnostic accuracy | STARD | Explicit threshold and reference-standard reporting |
| Animal study | ARRIVE | Species- and intervention-specific guidance |
| Qualitative study | COREQ or SRQR | Named analytic framework and reflexivity statement |
| Machine-learning benchmark or engineering experiment | Venue checklist plus project contract | Dataset lineage, leakage tests, baseline parity, seeds, uncertainty, compute, code/environment and failure cases |

For each applicable item, keep: checklist/item identifier, manuscript location, exact evidence artifact, status, and unresolved rationale. Use the official checklist version and URL; do not infer compliance from fluent prose. The checklist must not erase negative results, limitations or deviations from preregistration.
