## Candidate 1 — Defensible vehicle-electrification decisions under evolving cost evidence

**Chinese working label:** 变化成本证据下的电动车全生命周期决策稳健性

**Doctoral question.** When do public cost and usage inputs justify a stable recommendation to electrify a vehicle or fleet, and when should uncertainty, source age or structural missingness force a conditional decision or further information gathering?

**Pilot working title:** *Do Electric-Vehicle Cost Rankings Survive Data-Vintage Changes and Joint Input Uncertainty?*

### Motivation and closest evidence

This candidate connects mechanical-system performance with the researcher's reported cost-estimation software and BI experience. Its object is the defensibility of a decision under incomplete evidence, rather than another TCO calculator.

The indexed text for [Comparing costs and climate impacts of various electric vehicle charging systems across the United States, 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-024-49157-5) describes TCO and emissions comparisons with optimistic/baseline/conservative input scenarios. The full page failed to open, so its exact uncertainty treatment, code and supplementary assumptions are not verified here. The indexed abstract of [Data-driven analysis of strategic–operational interfaces in freight electrification under deep uncertainty, 2025](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1361920924004814), DOI 10.1016/j.trd.2024.104524, already connects fleet-operation representation, TCO and robust electrification decisions. [Correcting market failure for no-regret electric road investments under uncertainty, 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-62679-w) is another close decision/uncertainty lead, read only as a search excerpt.

These works make “TCO plus scenarios” an existing approach. The proposed difference is a testable treatment of **which source vintages and joint uncertainty structures can support a decision certificate**, including an explicit “insufficient evidence” outcome. Whether that difference is original remains unresolved until full methods, supplementary models and adjacent robust-decision literature are compared.

### Focal hypothesis and competing explanations

**H1:** A decision certificate that binds source vintages and a defensible joint input set can distinguish stable cost preferences from fragile preferences more informatively than a single point estimate or independent one-at-a-time sensitivity analysis, without obtaining its apparent reliability solely by withholding nearly every recommendation.

Observable predictions are: some apparent technology preferences change when information available at the decision date is respected; a source-bound certificate identifies those fragile cases before later inputs are exposed; and useful recommendations remain for a nontrivial set of cases. These are hypotheses, not observed results.

Competing explanations:

1. Annual distance, vehicle class or purchase price explains the ranking entirely; vintage and dependence modeling add no useful information.
2. Reversals are produced by unrealistic uncertainty ranges, unit/currency mistakes, incentive mixing or mismatched vehicle capability.
3. A wide uncertainty set trivially contains every outcome, making the certificate uninformative rather than scientifically better.

The discriminating comparison must match vehicle service, ownership horizon, discounting convention and information date. Compare a deterministic calculator, independent sensitivity/scenario analysis, a simple interval rule, and the proposed joint certificate. Report certified-case fraction and worst-case/held-out regret separately. Never treat scenario-frequency counts as empirical probabilities without an evidenced sampling model.

### Smallest falsification pilot

Freeze the price/efficiency/usage definitions and source dates before computing results. Test a small declared set of mileage and charging-use conditions, including a boundary case, with correlated energy-price variation justified by public time series or conservative dependence bounds.

The first test asks whether source-vintage handling changes any defensible recommendation after the capability and accounting checks. **Continue** if a reproducible decision reversal or inadequacy survives those checks and a source-bound rule predicts its vulnerability without becoming vacuous. **Revise** if only a narrower cost component or usage stratum is identifiable. **Stop the proposed vintage mechanism** for that setting if rankings remain stable throughout the supported input set, or if every reversal requires unsupported inputs. A stable result is a valid negative result; it does not by itself supply six papers.

This pilot measures sensitivity and conditional decision behavior. Without observed acquisition/operating outcomes, it cannot validate realized fleet savings or claim causal effects of electrification.

## Candidate 2 — Dependable maintenance decisions with delayed prognostic feedback

**Chinese working label:** 延迟反馈与工况变化下的机械寿命预测决策可靠性

**Doctoral question.** How can a maintenance-support system distinguish a calibration problem, a prediction-model failure and insufficient evidence when operating regimes change and independent failure labels arrive slowly?

**Pilot working title:** *When Should a Prognostic Model Recalibrate, Retrain or Withhold a Maintenance Recommendation?*

### Motivation and closest evidence

This uses the mechanical/data-analysis background and avoids laboratory acquisition or large-model training. Its unit of inference is a whole physical/simulated asset trajectory, not thousands of dependent windows presented as independent replications.

[Empirical Calibration and Conditional-Reliability Diagnostics for Bearing RUL Prediction under Operating-Regime Shift, Yang, Wang and Wang, 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08273v1) explicitly studies held-out load/speed regimes, interval calibration, conditional failures and raw-channel loss on a processed 10-bearing subset. I read its actual abstract, not its 36-page methods or code. This is direct counterevidence to a broad novelty claim for regime-aware RUL reliability. The older [Conformal Prediction Intervals for Remaining Useful Lifetime Estimation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.14612), encountered as an indexed abstract excerpt, already applies conformal methods to C-MAPSS. The 2026 [Conformal Repair on a Budget](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v329/lofstrom26b.html) search excerpt compares recalibration and model updating in a controlled linear setting. It challenges a generic “choose recalibration versus refitting” contribution.

The narrower candidate asks about **asset-level dependence, delayed/selected outcome availability and maintenance-action consequences together**. Their joint treatment may still already exist in survival, online calibration, selective prediction or predictive-maintenance literature. No novelty determination has been made.

### Focal hypothesis and competing explanations

**H2:** At a fixed declared recommendation/abstention rate and comparable information availability, an asset-aware repair policy can reduce harmful late-maintenance recommendations under held-out operating conditions compared with pooled residual recalibration and indiscriminate retraining.

Candidate policy actions are do nothing, recalibrate a fixed predictor, update the predictor, or withhold a recommendation. Any cost-sensitive action evaluation must use recorded public costs or explicitly hypothetical cost-ratio sensitivity; it cannot create observed maintenance savings.

Competing explanations:

1. Improvements come from an easier asset split, target truncation, condition normalization or leaked future endpoints.
2. Any method looks reliable by widening intervals or abstaining on most assets.
3. A well-tuned ordinary predictor plus simple asset-block calibration suffices; a new repair selector adds nothing.
4. Failure labels appear selectively after maintenance, so apparent repair performance is an observation-policy artifact.

Compare at matched recommendation fractions and interval widths where meaningful; report the trade-off frontier if exact matching is impossible. Preserve performance by independent asset and operating condition. Window-level sample size must not determine inferential confidence.

### Smallest falsification pilot

Start with a simple ridge/gradient-boosting baseline on a declared NASA C-MAPSS subset. Split **engine IDs** before window construction; preserve chronological feature availability and separate training, tuning, calibration and test assets. Use an actual recorded operating-condition contrast within the data; do not call FD001-versus-FD002 a clean causal intervention because fault/condition composition must first be verified.

Use nominal 90% intervals as a proposed common target, then compare pooled calibration, asset-block calibration and one predeclared repair/withhold rule. Artificially delay labels only as a labeled simulation, with a fixed observation policy. Measure interval coverage/width, recommendation rate and late-action risk across independent trajectories. Unit-level resampling is appropriate only when enough independent units exist; otherwise retain descriptive uncertainty.

**Continue** if a non-vacuous action advantage survives asset separation, equal information and the simple calibration competitor. **Revise** if the finding is an evaluation defect or a particular regime boundary rather than a new selector. **Stop the selector mechanism** if the advantage disappears under those controls or cannot be distinguished with available assets. A failure of conventional guarantees under dependence is not itself a new theorem.

## Candidate 3 — Assumption-sensitive safety certificates for longitudinal autonomy

**Chinese working label:** 观测误差与模型失配下的自动驾驶安全证书边界

**Doctoral question.** Under what observable sensing, dynamics and intervention assumptions can a longitudinal safety certificate remain informative, and when should an explicit counterexample or monitor declare that its guarantee no longer applies?

**Pilot working title:** *From Trajectory Replay to Closed-Loop Counterexamples: Stress-Testing Longitudinal Barrier Certificates*

### Motivation and closest evidence

This addresses mechanical dynamics and formal reliability without a robot laboratory or perception-model training. The research object is a precise boundary on an assurance claim, rather than generic autonomous-driving performance.

The checkpoint already includes [Formal Safety Guarantees for Autonomous Vehicles using Barrier Certificates, Barhoumi, Zaki and Tahar, 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.09740v1). The actual abstract and HTML introduction describe TTC-linked barriers and SMT verification; the case-study heading identifies highD. The complete mathematical conditions and case-study procedures were not read. Consequently, this draft does **not** assert that the paper makes a replay/closed-loop error or has an invalid proof.

The search also located [When Environments Shift: Safe Planning with Generative Priors and Robust Conformal Prediction, 2026](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v331/rahaman26a.html), read only as an indexed metadata/abstract excerpt. The checkpoint abstract for [Learning Multiplicative Interactions with Bayesian Neural Networks for Visual-Inertial Odometry, 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.07630v1) provides an adjacent sensing-failure lead, not a directly comparable longitudinal certificate. A proper closest-work set must include sampled-data barrier methods, robust reachability, delayed sensing and runtime assurance. That search is incomplete.

### Focal hypothesis and competing explanations

**H3:** An assumption-aware certificate or monitor can distinguish failures caused by discrete observation, uncertain acceleration/braking limits and intervention delay, while preserving useful feasible interventions better than a nominal TTC-based rule evaluated only at observed samples.

Competing explanations:

1. Any apparent safety improvement is a consequence of assuming a nonreactive leading vehicle or evaluating a changed ego action against unchanged recorded trajectories.
2. A simple braking-distance rule already provides the same useful boundary at much lower complexity.
3. The new monitor is safe only by declaring nearly every interaction infeasible.
4. Counterexamples violate the specified physical or sensing envelope and therefore do not challenge the actual certificate.

Separate mathematical guarantee, numerical solver outcome and empirical diagnostic. An unsatisfiable query proves only its encoded property under its assumptions and numerical/logic semantics. Low TTC is a surrogate, not an observed collision count.

### Smallest falsification pilot

Implement a minimal two-vehicle longitudinal simulator with explicit distance/speed state, bounded lead acceleration, ego braking/actuation bounds, discrete sampling and observation error. First verify analytic boundary cases and the consistency of units/signs. Compare a simple emergency-braking rule, a nominal TTC/barrier rule and one explicitly robust formulation.

Construct a declared grid of sampling/delay/error cases. Search for counterexamples near the boundary, then rerun each in a deterministic closed-loop simulator in which an intervention actually changes the future ego state. Keep the assumed leading-vehicle response explicit. Use a small set of public trajectory segments only to initialize states and characterize observed conditions; observed human trajectories do not supply counterfactual controlled outcomes.

**Continue** if a counterexample within the declared envelope exposes a meaningful missing condition and the proposed condition/monitor resolves it while allowing useful intervention. **Revise** if the issue is merely implementation or a choice of safety surrogate. **Stop the claimed new mechanism** if an existing robust braking/barrier formulation already explains the boundary, or the only successful policy is operationally vacuous. A simulation result remains conditional on the simulator.
